## 1. Brand voice and story

**Uling** (Filipino for charcoal) is a modern Kapampangan grill and bar: heirloom recipes from Pampanga's kitchens, cooked over live coals, served in a warm, loud, generous room.

**Tagline:** Kapampangan, over the coals.

**Voice:** warm, proud, a little playful. Talk like a host who's genuinely excited for you to eat. Short sentences. A Kapampangan word now and then, always explained in context.

| Do | Don't |
| --- | --- |
| "Our sisig is chopped to order and hits the plate still crackling." | "Experience exquisite culinary artistry." |
| "Bring the whole barkada." | "We cater to discerning guests." |
| Name the dish, the place and the feeling | Stack adjectives |
| Kapampangan words with context: "pindang damulag, our cured carabao tapa" | Untranslated jargon |

**Kapampangan touches to use sparingly:** *Masanting* (beautiful, great), *Mangan ta na!* (let's eat!), *Kayabe* (together with), *Dinatang* (you've arrived). Have a Kapampangan speaker check these before launch.

### Founder story (sample)

Uling began in Lola Pacing's kitchen in San Fernando, where Sunday lunch meant a charcoal grill in the yard and twelve cousins fighting over the crispy bits. Her grandson, Chef Migo Dizon, cooked in Manila and Singapore for a decade before coming home with one question: what would her table taste like today?

The answer is Uling. We source pork, carabao milk and river fish from Pampanga farms, make our own buro and atchara, and cook almost everything over coals. Some recipes are exactly as Lola wrote them. Others we've dared to change. Either way, you'll eat like family.

*Lola Pacing and Chef Migo are fictional characters for this concept.*

## 7. Photos without real materials

Use free stock photos for most shots, your own phone photos for the Kapampangan dishes stock doesn't cover, and illustration to carry the brand. Credit sources in the footer: *"Photography: Unsplash, Pexels, Wikimedia Commons contributors and the designer; illustrations original."*

### Your options

| Source | Best for | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Your own photos | 4–6 signature dishes you cook or buy, ingredients, textures | Ask permission if you shoot inside a real restaurant, and don't show its branding |
| Free stock (Unsplash, Pexels) | Textures, charcoal, banana leaves, generic Filipino spreads | Thin coverage of Kapampangan dishes, so many sites reuse the same shots. Read each license. |
| Illustration and SVG | Section dividers, map, icons, 404, empty states | Keep one consistent line weight and palette |

**No AI-generated images.** Use only free stock, Wikimedia Commons or the designer's own photos and illustration.

**Never** use images from real restaurants' websites, Instagram or Google Maps listings.

### Phone photography tips for your own shots

- Shoot near a window in daylight; turn off overhead lights.
- Two angles per dish: overhead for spreads, 45° for bowls and drinks.
- Props: dark wood, banana leaf, clay pots, enamel plates, calamansi, chilies.
- Add steam or sizzle by shooting the moment the dish lands; a hand reaching in adds life.
- Edit all shots with one preset so they match the stock photos (warm, slightly low-key).

### Art direction

Warm ember light, deep shadows, dark wood and terracotta, banana-leaf green accents, shallow depth of field, food slightly messy and generous. No stark white backgrounds.

### Shot list and free stock sources

**Where to search:**

- **Unsplash** and **Pexels** — free for commercial use with no attribution required; best for interiors, grills, textures and generic Filipino spreads.
- **Pixabay** — a third pool when the first two run dry.
- **Wikimedia Commons** — the best chance of finding specific Kapampangan dishes (sisig, kare-kare, halo-halo). Licenses vary (often CC BY or CC BY-SA), so credit the photographer exactly as the file page asks. Quality is uneven.

**How to pick:** filter by orientation before you browse; reject anything with visible logos, signage or brand packaging; favor warm, low-key shots so the set matches; save each photo's URL and photographer in a credits sheet as you go. Kapampangan-specific dishes are thinly covered, so expect to shoot sisig, kare-kare and buro yourself.
